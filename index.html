About

Benji is an American-born Israeli scholar and educator whose work bridges research and practice in Israel and Jewish education.

He teaches from middle school through doctoral level, and has taught campers, gap year students, campus professionals, clergy, lay leaders and donors across the religious and political spectrum. Before his 2025 appointment at Yeshiva University, Benji received the Harold Wechsler Award for Emerging Scholars from the Network for Research in Jewish Education and held the Israel Institute Teaching and Research Fellowship in Israel Studies at George Mason University.

Consulting

I help you root Israel and Jewish education in the area of scholarship that fits your context, connect you to the organizations already doing the work, and, where nothing exists yet, design and build a plan of action.

01 · First visionClarify the visionName the Jewish why, the learners, and the education goals that matter most.

This is partnership over time, from first conversation to lasting change: articulate a vision for Israel and Jewish education in your setting, build out a plan, and hone the craft of the educators who will carry it.

Speaking Topics

23 sessions in three tracks. Pick one for an engaging talk, or build a series of sessions.

Israel, Zionism and the Jewish World

9 sessions

  • What are the main challenges facing Israeli society in the coming decades? An analysis of societal trends, issues within the Jewish world, and long-term national security threats.

  • The purpose of Israel and its role in the lives of Jews outside Israel. Participants consider the idea of Jews as a nation and reflect on their own connection to Israel.

  • Why is one people’s Jewishness rooted in ethno-nationalism and another in liberal-religious values? How the two largest Jewish communities understand what it means to be Jewish in the 21st century.

  • A breakdown of the Israeli political system, its pros and cons, where the political currents are flowing, and what the political map may look like in the future.

  • The challenge of Israel being a Jewish state for all Jews of different streams, beliefs and backgrounds. Should buses run on Shabbat in Tel Aviv? Does the future of Torah study require an army exemption for certain yeshiva students? Can the Western Wall be welcoming to all denominations?

  • The narratives in Israeli society for and against the role of religion in government and broader society, and its place in the Israel/Diaspora relationship.

  • Why are the United States and Israeli governments so close? The many layers of the relationship, and where it may be headed.

  • Israel functions as a compromise between different Jewish and non-Jewish groups: secular Jews, national religious Jews, ultra-Orthodox Jews and Arabs. These compromises and their impact on Israeli society today.

  • What is the Zionist idea, and what are its roots? A brief history of Jewish nationalism from antiquity to today.

Conflict and Geopolitics

10 sessions

  • A deep dive into how Jews and Arabs, Israelis and Palestinians, perceive the same land as their own, exploring historical, theological and national claims.

  • What are the “territories”? Why do some say they are occupied, while others say they are disputed or liberated? What are Areas A, B and C? A short introduction to the West Bank / Judea and Samaria.

  • A historical introduction to the century-long conflict between Arabs and Jews in the Holy Land.

  • How does today’s news fit into the local political context and the greater geopolitical challenges and opportunities facing Israel?

  • Why is the land Israel conquered, liberated or occupied in 1967 still not sovereign Israeli territory? Israel’s relationship with the West Bank and its implications for politics, security and Zionist identity.

  • Three major ways to frame the Israeli-Palestinian conflict: geopolitics, social justice and tribal identity. Participants explore each lens and what it implies for meaningful discussion and solutions.

  • Why do most people focus on the Israeli-Palestinian conflict when Israel’s current war is against Iran and its proxies? A shift of focus to Israel’s broader security concerns.

  • Political, military and diplomatic. Israeli leaders weigh many factors, from restoring deterrence to maintaining regional power. Israel’s strategic objectives across all three dimensions.

  • How Israelis and Palestinians view the wars of 1948 and 1967 differently: one as securing independence, the other as catastrophe and occupation.

  • People love to say the conflict is complex. Some elements genuinely are, others are quite simple. This session helps learners tell the difference.

The Israel Conversation and Antisemitism

4 sessions

  • Many get confused about the boundary between legitimate criticism of Israel and outright anti-Zionism. A look at criticism of Israel within the Jewish community, and the line between critique and delegitimization.

  • Antisemitism was once thought to be a thing of the past, yet it persists. The historical, societal and political contexts of its rise in the West, why it is unique and persistent, and what can be done about it.

  • One of today’s most polarizing and complex topics. A model for engaging with different paradigms and political lenses, and an introduction to the “Rootedness and Respect” approach for productive discussion.

  • Why do young Jewish progressives feel pressured to check their Zionism at the door in social justice spaces? The philosophy, ethics and culture behind this phenomenon.

Workshops

Professional development for faculties, staff teams and educator cohorts, built on my research

  • Israel education is not one thing. This workshop lays out the competing answers to what it is for, and what each one asks of a teacher. Educators leave able to name the model they are actually working in, and to choose it on purpose.

    From Israel education: A philosophical analysis, Journal of Jewish Education

  • Complexity has become the default answer in Israel education. This workshop asks what it actually requires: teaching Israel as a sensitive subject rather than a controversial one, rooted in a Zionist vision, without giving up on complex narratives or on commitment to our Jewish identity.

    From Complicating complexity: Israel education after October 7, Sources · Teaching Palestinian perspectives and the pedagogy of “historical empathy”, in Education after October 7

  • Knowing Israel and knowing how to teach are two different things, and neither one on its own is enough. Built on my doctoral research, this workshop names the knowledge that sits between them and gives a team a way to build it.

    From “You never told me”: The pedagogical content knowledge of Israel education, Contemporary Jewry

  • Identity is usually treated as an outcome to produce. This workshop offers a different frame: identity education as an ethical practice, where the learner does the deciding and the educator is responsible for the conditions.

    From The PCK of ethical identity education, Oxford Review of Education · Liberal Jewish identity and the pedagogy of Israel education, Journal of Religious Education

  • What does it mean to belong to the Jewish people? A working session on teaching Jewish identity through the paradigm of land, language and culture: Israel, Hebrew and Judaism.

    From Am Yisrael Chai: Education for belonging to a “living Jewish people”, The Jewish Education Project · Israel education in a post-October 7th world, Jewish Educational Leadership

  • Zionism taught from within, as a sacred commitment with values and ideas of its own. And alongside it, what is different, because no one can embrace something as sacred without also meeting the alternatives. Educators leave able to hold both, so that learners make their own choices about what it means to be a Zionist.

    From Hanan Alexander, Mature Zionism: Education and the scholarly study of Israel, Journal of Jewish Education · Israel education: A philosophical analysis, Journal of Jewish Education · “You never told me”, Contemporary Jewry · Liberal Jewish identity and the pedagogy of Israel education, Journal of Religious Education
